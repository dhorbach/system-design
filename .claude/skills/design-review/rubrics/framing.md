# Pass: framing

Every section earns its place and tells the reader what it is doing before it does it.

## Check

1. **Low-value framing.** Opening sections (Problem, Overview, Introduction) contain only what a reader needs to understand the problem: what the system does, for whom, and why it is hard. Flag sentences that announce structure ("the features fit in one sentence", "the next section explains"), restate a heading, or promise content without giving any.
2. **Context before results.** A section that presents numbers, tables or a diagram first says what is being worked out and why it matters. Flag a section whose first sentence is a result ("750 times the average") before the reader knows what is being measured.
3. **Tables and figures introduced.** Two adjacent tables, or a table followed by formulas, need a sentence saying how they relate (inputs and outputs, before and after).

Depth levels (`data-l`) are checked by the simplicity pass, and term definitions by the terminology pass; do not report them here.

## Evidence

Quote the low-value sentence, or the first sentence of the section that lacks context.

## Severity

- high: a reader cannot tell what a section is for.
- medium: a section opens with results before context; filler in the Problem section.
- low: a single transitional sentence that could go.
